Sports Betting Calculator Suite

Six working calculators for the maths behind a bet: converting odds, reading what odds imply, checking expected value, sizing stakes with the Kelly criterion, splitting a two-way arbitrage, and pricing a parlay. Every result shows its working, step by step.

Read this first: no tool on this page predicts winners — and neither does any “AI predictor.” There is no model here and no tip hidden in the numbers. The odds a bookmaker posts are the market’s own estimate of how likely each outcome is, with the bookmaker’s margin built in.

What these calculators actually do is narrower and more honest: they convert odds between formats, show you the probability the odds imply, test whether your own estimate beats the price on offer, and size stakes sensibly. Where a calculator asks for a win probability, that number must come from you — the page never supplies one.

      Expected value (EV) calculator

      Tests a price against your own probability estimate. Positive EV means that if your estimate is right and you could repeat the bet many times, you would profit on average. It says nothing about any single bet.

      Working

        In plain words: EV weighs the two outcomes by how likely you think they are: (chance of winning × profit if you win) minus (chance of losing × stake). The catch is the input — if your probability estimate is wrong, the EV figure is wrong with it. Most bettors overestimate their edge; the odds’ break-even probability is the hurdle your honest estimate has to clear.

        Kelly criterion stake calculator

        Given a bankroll and your own probability estimate, Kelly sizes the stake that grows a bankroll fastest in theory. Full Kelly is aggressive and swings hard, so half- and quarter-Kelly stakes are shown alongside — most people who use Kelly at all use a fraction of it.

        Working

          In plain words: Kelly bets more when your edge is bigger and the odds are shorter, and nothing at all when your estimate is at or below break-even. Its weakness is the same as EV’s: it trusts your probability completely. Overstate your chance by a few points and Kelly will tell you to stake far too much — which is exactly why fractional Kelly exists.

          Two-way arbitrage calculator

          If two bookmakers price the same two-outcome event differently enough, backing both sides in the right proportions returns the same amount whichever side wins. This finds the split — and tells you plainly when no arbitrage exists.

          Working

            In plain words: each side’s stake is set in inverse proportion to its odds, so both outcomes pay the same total. The catch in real life: prices move in seconds, bookmakers can void bets or limit accounts, and one leg can fail to place — leaving you exposed on the other. The maths locks in a profit only if both bets are actually accepted at these prices.

            Working

              In plain words: three even-money legs at 2.00 each don’t make a 6.00 shot that’s “a bit harder” — they make an 8.00 shot the odds themselves price at a 12.5% chance, and each leg carries the bookmaker’s margin, so the margins compound too. That is why parlays feel generous and pay out rarely.

              Bet responsibly

              These calculators do arithmetic, not miracles. The bookmaker’s margin means most bettors lose money over time, and no staking formula — Kelly included — turns a losing approach into a winning one. Only ever bet money you can afford to lose entirely, never chase losses, and never bet to solve a money problem.

              If betting stops being entertainment, help is free and confidential: BeGambleAware.org (UK), Gambling Help Online (Australia), the National Problem Gambling Helpline 1-800-522-4700 (US), or search for your country’s gambling support service. You must be of legal gambling age in your jurisdiction to bet.
